# Franklin — Channel Bible

## One-sentence premise

Franklin explains the machinery behind money, markets, and economic history with clear causal reasoning, dry humour, and the occasional gaming analogy.

## Viewer promise

After ten to fifteen minutes, the viewer should understand a system well enough to explain it to somebody else, recognize where common explanations go wrong, and know which assumptions could change the conclusion.

## Audience

The primary viewer is financially curious and already has basic investing knowledge. They can follow terms such as stock, bond, inflation, central bank, recession, and index fund. Anything more technical should be introduced through use.

The audience is intelligent but busy. Do not confuse accessibility with childishness, and do not confuse expertise with jargon density.

## Public identity

The public host is **Franklin**, a stylized Benjamin Franklin portrait based on the $100 bill. Videos use mascot-led voice-over rather than an on-camera presenter.

The creator's degrees, employer, job title, and career history are not part of the branding. Scripts should earn trust through reasoning, sourcing, and intellectual honesty. Never write “as a finance professional,” “from my time in banking,” or similar credential-based framing.

## Franklin's character

Franklin is:

- composed, observant, and mildly skeptical;
- fascinated by the strange incentives inside ordinary systems;
- willing to admit when evidence is mixed;
- dry rather than loud;
- confident about well-supported mechanics and careful about interpretation.

Franklin is not:

- a guru, trader caricature, or get-rich-quick mascot;
- permanently cynical about institutions;
- an omniscient historian;
- a substitute for the creator's argument;
- a meme pasted over weak writing.

Mascot reactions should be restrained: a glance, a raised eyebrow, a small prop, a deadpan pause, or a visual callback. One precise reaction is stronger than continuous animation.

## Content pillars

1. **How financial systems work** — interest rates, bonds, banking, money creation, derivatives, credit, risk, market plumbing.
2. **Economics hidden in ordinary life** — pricing, incentives, insurance, housing, labour, queues, subscriptions, shortages, and consumer behaviour.
3. **Financial history with a mechanism** — crises, bubbles, policy regimes, corporate failures, currency events, and institutional inventions.
4. **Games as miniature economies** — occasional examples from virtual markets, loot systems, auction houses, resource sinks, inflation, and player incentives. Finance remains the subject; the game supplies a useful model.

## Editorial position

- Explain incentives without reducing every human decision to money.
- Institutions are neither automatically wise nor automatically corrupt. Examine the rule, incentive, information, and constraint.
- Markets are useful coordination systems with identifiable failure modes. Avoid worship and blanket dismissal.
- Economic models are tools with assumptions. State what a model clarifies and what it leaves out.
- Historical outcomes were uncertain to the people living through them. Avoid hindsight inevitability.

## Default format

- English-language narrated essay.
- 10–15 minutes unless the idea earns a different length.
- One central question and one central thesis.
- Calm visual pacing with selective energy in the opening and key reveals.
- Evidence shown on screen: charts, official documents, archival material, simplified diagrams, contemporary reporting, and clean numerical examples.
- Franklin appears at transitions, punchlines, and explanatory moments rather than filling every frame.

## Boundaries

- No stock-picking identity, portfolio flexing, trading signals, or guaranteed-return framing.
- No partisan team-sport treatment of economics.
- No current-event rush that sacrifices verification.
- No disclosure or fictionalization of confidential professional information.
- No jokes aimed at victims of crises, poverty, unemployment, fraud, or financial loss.
- No gaming reference that requires the viewer to know the game in order to understand the finance.
