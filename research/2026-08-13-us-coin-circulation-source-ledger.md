# Franklin Source and Claim Ledger — America Had Enough Coins. It Still Ran Out.

## Project Metadata

- **Topic:** The 2020–2022 U.S. coin circulation disruption
- **Research question:** How could retailers lack change when the country possessed billions of dollars in coins?
- **Working thesis:** Pandemic-era closures and payment changes broke the return paths that normally supplied most usable coin, while retail demand recovered faster than deposits and new production.
- **Researcher:** Franklin project
- **Started:** 13 August 2026
- **Freshness cutoff:** 13 August 2026
- **Dossier:** [2026-08-13-us-coin-circulation-disruption.md](./2026-08-13-us-coin-circulation-disruption.md)

## Calculation Sheet

### Calculation 1 — Reconstructing 2019 Federal Reserve flows

- **Purpose:** Reconcile rounded Task Force figures with the Federal Reserve’s 2020 year-over-year percentages.
- **Inputs:** 2020 payments 51.9bn, down 24.1%; receipts 34.0bn, down 39.4%.
- **Payments formula:** 51.9 / 0.759 = 68.38bn.
- **Receipts formula:** 34.0 / 0.606 = 56.11bn.
- **Result:** Approximately 68.4bn paid and 56.1bn received in 2019.
- **Rounding:** One decimal billion pieces.
- **Sanity check:** Aligns with State of Coin’s rounded 68bn payment figure.

### Calculation 2 — Calendar-2020 return ratio

- **Purpose:** Prevent the acute four-per-ten ratio from being presented as a full-year average.
- **Inputs:** 34.0bn receipts and 51.9bn payments.
- **Formula:** 34.0 / 51.9 = 65.5%.
- **Result:** Approximately 6.6 coins received per ten paid over the full calendar year.
- **Interpretation:** The acute return ratio was worse; later recovery lifts the annual average.

### Calculation 3 — Mint shipment increase

- **Inputs:** FY2020 15.479bn; FY2019 12.466bn.
- **Formula:** (15.479 − 12.466) / 12.466 = 24.17%.
- **Result:** 24.2%, matching the Mint annual report.
- **Caveat:** Fiscal-year production cannot be directly subtracted from calendar-year Fed flows.

### Calculation 4 — Deposit decline in later joint study

- **Inputs:** 4.7bn average monthly deposits in 2019; 2.4bn during pandemic.
- **Formula:** (2.4 − 4.7) / 4.7 = −48.9%.
- **Result:** Approximately 49% decline.
- **Caveat:** The report’s pandemic comparison window is not a single explicitly named calendar year.

## Competing Explanations

| Explanation | Best supporting evidence | Best objection | Current assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mint production failure | Initial output fell up to 35% | Full-year shipments increased 24.2%; new coin was only 17% of normal flow | Secondary contributor |
| Consumer panic hoarding | Jars grew and redemption fell | Much dormancy was passive; return channels were closed or inconvenient | Avoid “panic” framing |
| Bank hoarding | Precautionary inventory increased | Estimated 2–3bn pieces versus 15–25bn circulation decline | Amplifier |
| Falling cash use | Cash payments and small transactions fell | Coin supply returned even faster than coin demand fell | Important mechanism through return paths |
| Fed rationing | Caps constrained bank orders | Caps followed depleted inventory | Response |
| Hygiene fear | Consumers and merchants avoided touch | Contribution is not separately identified | Supporting factor only |
| No shortage at all | Aggregate stock was ample | Usable inventory was unavailable where needed | Aggregate abundance and local shortage coexist |

## Research Handoff

### Verified

- Aggregate coin remained ample while accessible wholesale and retail inventories became scarce.
- Recirculation was the majority source of normal coin flow.
- Deposit collapse plus reopening demand created the acute imbalance.
- Mint production and reserve releases mitigated but did not immediately solve the problem.
- Allocation dates and first/second-wave chronology are well supported.
- Cash inclusion is a legitimate consequence, with FDIC data for scale.

### Contested

- Exact national coin-jar value.
- Relative causal weight of hygiene concerns.
- Degree of precautionary inventory accumulation at banks and retailers.
- Whether all eroded return pathways are permanently structural.

### Unresolved

- High-frequency denomination and regional data.
- Exact second-wave allocation end dates by denomination.
- Full underlying proprietary survey and stakeholder datasets used in the Task Force reports.

### Do Not Say

- $48 billion means 48 billion coins.
- The Mint ceased production.
- The Federal Reserve issues U.S. coin.
- The entire country had no change.
- The shortage was caused by one irrational actor.
- The annual return ratio was four per ten.
- The 2020 Mint and Fed totals use identical reporting periods.
