## Seed ideas

### Inflation Can Fall While Prices Keep Rising

- **Pillar:** Familiar concept, missing mechanism
- **Status:** spark
- **Central question:** Why does “inflation is down” feel false at the supermarket?
- **Provisional thesis:** Falling inflation describes a slower rate of price increase, while household experience also depends on the accumulated price level, wage growth, and the personal consumption basket.
- **Why somebody clicks:** It resolves a common argument that appears whenever inflation data improve before living costs feel normal.
- **Visual anchor:** Two clean lines: inflation rate falling while the price-level index continues upward.
- **Freshness risk:** low if the episode uses durable mechanics rather than one release.

### Why Bond Prices Move Backwards

- **Pillar:** Familiar concept, missing mechanism
- **Status:** spark
- **Central question:** Why does an existing bond lose value when new interest rates rise?
- **Provisional thesis:** The price move is the market's way of making fixed old cash flows compete with newly available cash flows; duration measures how exposed those old payments are to the repricing.
- **Why somebody clicks:** Many investors know the rule but cannot derive it.
- **Visual anchor:** A $100 future payment sliding down in present value as the discount rate rises.
- **Freshness risk:** low.

### A Bank Run Can Be Rational for Everyone

- **Pillar:** Historical event, reusable model
- **Status:** spark
- **Central question:** Why can individually sensible withdrawals destroy a bank that might otherwise survive?
- **Provisional thesis:** Maturity transformation makes confidence part of a bank's operating structure; deposit insurance and central-bank liquidity change the game by changing the depositor's payoff.
- **Why somebody clicks:** It turns panic from a story about irrational crowds into a coordination problem.
- **Gaming reference, only if useful:** A raid group abandoning a recoverable attempt because each player expects everyone else to leave.
- **Visual anchor:** A simple depositor payoff matrix followed by an archival queue.
- **Freshness risk:** low.

### Where Bank Money Actually Comes From

- **Pillar:** Familiar concept, missing mechanism
- **Status:** spark
- **Central question:** Does a bank lend out money that somebody else deposited first?
- **Provisional thesis:** Bank lending generally creates a loan asset and matching deposit, while capital, liquidity, funding cost, regulation, credit demand, and settlement constrain how far the balance sheet can expand.
- **Why somebody clicks:** The popular explanations are usually simple, confident, and incomplete in opposite directions.
- **Visual anchor:** A balance sheet that expands on both sides in one clean animation.
- **Freshness risk:** low; jurisdictional details require care.

### The Game Economy Problem Central Banks Do Not Have

- **Pillar:** Virtual economy, real lesson
- **Status:** spark
- **Central question:** Why can a game developer fight inflation by deleting currency while a central bank cannot be understood as the administrator of a giant game economy?
- **Provisional thesis:** Faucets and sinks explain part of monetary supply, but national economies add bank credit, production, expectations, distribution, exchange rates, and political institutions that make the game analogy break quickly.
- **Why somebody clicks:** The gaming comparison is intuitive, then the limitations teach the deeper economics.
- **Visual anchor:** A game-style faucet and sink expanding into a map of banks, firms, households, and government.
- **Freshness risk:** low.
